# Honey Badger Guide | Instructor edition

> **EDUCATIONAL MATERIAL ONLY — NOTHING IN THIS DOCUMENT IS LEGAL ADVICE.** Seek a qualified, licensed Iowa attorney for any clarification or individual legal question. Verify current law and court orders. Independent project; no affiliation or endorsement. All simulated cases are fictional. Do not file classroom materials in court.


12 sessions • 90–120 minutes each • 2026

Independent education, not legal advice or court-approved training. Use fictional examples only. No actual filing, contact with opposing parties or collection activity in class. Consult official current law and an Iowa attorney for individualized questions. Materials assume ordinary money claims unless stated otherwise.

Begin with Lesson 0 — Pro Se in Iowa (see docs/00-pro-se.md).

## Teaching method and assessment

Prerequisites: basic reading and arithmetic; no legal experience. Provide printed or accessible electronic materials, an offline alternative to web research, and extra time as needed. Each session: 10-minute retrieval review, 25-minute concept lesson, 15-minute source-reading demonstration, 30-minute scenario exercise, 10-minute discussion and exit quiz. Optional 30-minute extension for independent source research. Use the student handbook and matching worksheet. Assess reasoning rather than confidence or advocacy skill. Never ask learners to disclose personal disputes.

Suggested grading: unit quizzes 30%, worksheets 30%, capstone 40%. Suggested mastery: 80% overall with remediation of deadline, service, privacy and enforcement errors regardless of score. No legal qualification or accreditation is conferred.

## Lesson 1 | Court structure and roles

### Learning outcomes

Distinguish civil from criminal cases; identify plaintiff, defendant, clerk and judicial officer.

### Opening scenario

A neighbor claims $180 for damaged property. Who decides liability? Who serves process?

### Teaching and practice

Ask learners to read the matching student-handbook section and locate the controlling authority. Model separating established facts, disputed facts, legal rules and unresolved questions. Guided activity: Draw the case lifecycle and label each actor. Explain why the clerk cannot advise either side. Pair students to critique each other's work; require a source and a reason for every procedural conclusion.

### Debrief and exit ticket

Have each learner name one action, one prerequisite, one uncertainty and the authority to verify. Use the matching quiz in the assessment pack. Correct any claim that an outcome is guaranteed. Source focus: Iowa Code ch. 631; Judicial Branch small claims guide.

## Capstone facilitation

Use the capstone packet. Assign plaintiff, defendant, neutral judicial officer and observers. Allow 20 minutes to prepare, 10 minutes per side, 10 minutes for questions and 10 minutes for findings and reflection. The exercise is not a real hearing. Assess issue identification, evidence links, arithmetic, procedural safeguards and civility; do not grade a learner by which side wins.
