## Part A | Case narrative

Alex Morgan purchased residential internet service. Service ended August 12, 2026. A final $96 payment cleared August 15; the parties agree that $72 was properly earned through termination and $24 should have been refunded. Morgan returned two devices at a staffed return location. The receipt lists device IDs TRAIN-A17 and TRAIN-B29. A subsequent invoice assessed $360 for unreturned equipment. A representative promised to reverse the charge; a later ledger shows an apparent equipment reversal followed by a $180 adjustment. Morgan disputes both the missing $24 refund and the resulting $180 balance. Morgan seeks only $24 in this simulated money claim, plus lawful costs and interest; whether a separate claim or remedy is available for the disputed invoice is a research question, including claim-preclusion risk.

## Part B | Simulated exhibits

### P1 — Service agreement excerpt

Residential service billed monthly; rented equipment must be returned within 30 days of termination. Charges for unreturned devices are $180 each. Refunds and adjustments are subject to the agreement. Dispute-resolution addendum not included; obtain the accepted version before deciding arbitration or notice obligations.

### P2 — Termination confirmation

August 12: “Service disconnected effective today. Please return both rented devices within 30 days.” Training account PL-0001.

### P3 — Payment and final service calculation

August 15: payment $96. Proper final service charges $72. Net refund due $24 (stipulated for classroom arithmetic; not a real judicial finding).

### P4 — Return receipt

August 19: TRAIN-A17 and TRAIN-B29 scanned at fictional return counter. Receipt status: received for processing. Does not establish later warehouse reconciliation.

### P5 — Invoice dated September 1

Prior credit −$24; equipment charges $360; net balance $336. Negative balance denotes credit owed to customer.

### P6 — Customer-support email September 3

“Both devices appear in the return log. I have requested reversal of the equipment charges. Please allow time for posting.” No promise of immediate payment is stated.

### P7 — Invoice dated October 1

Opening balance $336; equipment reversal −$360; closing-adjustment entry +$180; final balance $156. The $180 adjustment offsets part of the reversal and the existing $24 credit. Morgan disputes the adjustment; it has not been paid. A correct ledger with no adjustment would show −$24.

### P8 — Customer demand October 5

“Please explain the $180 entry, correct any erroneous balance, and refund the $24 credit. Please respond in writing.” This is not service of process.

### P9 — Automated notice October 18

“Balance $156 overdue. Please contact billing.” A notice does not prove intent or establish an independent cause of action.

### D1 — Defendant's warehouse extract

TRAIN-A17: received and reconciled. TRAIN-B29: intake scan recorded, warehouse assignment pending. Defendant says the adjustment represents a device still awaiting verification. The extract lacks serial-history and reconciliation detail.

### D2 — Defendant's statement

The $24 refund was queued, but payment status is not established. The employee explains the adjustment as an exception pending reconciliation and denies intentional wrongdoing. Defendant disputes whether Morgan proved a breach, when refund became due, and whether the proper entity was named. The parties may stipulate payment and dates for specific exercises, but must not assume other facts.
