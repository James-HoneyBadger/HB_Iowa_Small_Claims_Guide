## Quick orientation

Small claims is a division of Iowa district court, designed to resolve qualifying civil disputes through simplified procedures. A judge or magistrate, not a jury, decides the case. Winning and collecting are separate processes. The usual path is: evaluate the claim → identify parties and venue → file → obtain service → answer or default → hearing and judgment → appeal or other review if appropriate → voluntary payment or enforcement → satisfaction.

This guide focuses on ordinary money claims. Replevin (recovery of personal property), eviction and abandoned-property proceedings are also within specified small claims jurisdiction, but have distinct requirements. Do not apply the ordinary money-claim deadlines to those special proceedings. Start with Iowa Code chapter 631 and the official Judicial Branch small claims guide [1–2].

## 1 | Decide whether small claims is the right forum

### Jurisdiction, relief and parties

Under § 631.1, a money claim filed today may seek up to $6,500, excluding interest and costs. Qualifying replevin claims have a $6,500 property-value limit. Certain forcible entry and detainer actions under chapter 648 and abandonment proceedings under chapter 555B also qualify. Ordinary small claims jurisdiction is not a general license to obtain injunctions, broad declarations or orders requiring businesses to change their practices. If you need nonmonetary relief beyond the statutory categories, consult counsel about the correct forum. Do not artificially divide a single claim to evade the limit; claim preclusion may bar subsequent litigation.

Identify the person or legal entity actually liable, not merely a brand, trade name, parent company or registered agent. For an Iowa business, consult the Secretary of State’s business search [3], compare the contract and invoices, and confirm the current registered agent and service address. The agent receives process; the agent is not ordinarily the defendant. Verify venue and personal jurisdiction under § 631.2 and applicable venue statutes and civil rules. A county convenient to you is not automatically the correct county.

Individuals may represent themselves. Under § 631.14, an entity can generally appear through an officer or employee in small claims; residential rental property has additional representation provisions. This exception does not create a general right to represent others in regular district court proceedings. Seek counsel when authority to appear is uncertain.

### Limitations and pre-filing preparation

Identify the legal basis of the claim, each required element, and the applicable limitation period. Iowa Code chapter 614 contains common limitation periods; specialized statutes, accrual rules, tolling, government notice requirements and contract provisions can change the calculation. Do not assume a dispute, demand letter, agency complaint or negotiation pauses a filing deadline. Consult counsel before a potentially expiring deadline [4].

Practical preparation: collect the agreement, amendments, receipts, photographs, payment records and complete correspondence; build a dated chronology; compute the actual recoverable loss; identify witnesses with personal knowledge; and consider a concise written demand or settlement proposal. A demand is often useful but is not universally required. Preserve originals. Keep settlement communications separate from trial exhibits where admissibility may be restricted.

Example: a fictional customer paid $300 for an undelivered service and received a $100 refund. The remaining claimed principal is $200, subject to proof of liability and any defenses. Filing fees and allowable service costs are tracked separately; interest is requested when legally available. Do not count the same loss twice, invent penalties, or include time spent preparing the case without an independent legal basis.

## 2 | Contracts, arbitration and consumer classification

Read the actual agreement in effect for the transaction, including dispute-resolution provisions, governing law, forum selection, notice requirements and any small claims exception. Preserve the version and evidence of acceptance. A company-name field, business billing profile or payment card is evidence that may matter, but does not by itself determine legal consumer status. The transaction’s purpose, parties, contract and applicable law all matter.

The Federal Arbitration Act (9 U.S.C. §§ 2–4) and Iowa Code chapter 679A may require enforcement of a valid arbitration agreement. The applicable provider rules and contractual terms must be examined together. AAA Consumer Arbitration Rule R-9 contains a small claims option for qualifying disputes [5]. Do not assume AAA rules govern a JAMS clause, or that every commercial or consumer agreement contains the same exception. Consult the designated provider’s current rules and fee schedule [6].

Potential issues include whether a valid agreement was formed, whether the dispute is covered, whether the clause delegates threshold questions to an arbitrator, applicable small claims exceptions, enforceability and waiver. Morgan v. Sundance, Inc., 596 U.S. 411 (2022), rejects a special arbitration-specific prejudice requirement for waiver under federal law; it does not make every delay a waiver. A motion to compel may change the forum or stay proceedings. Do not ignore a motion or assume a small claims filing defeats arbitration. Seek legal advice promptly [7].

## 3 | File the case

Use the official court forms, not a homemade substitute where a prescribed form is required. The Iowa Judicial Branch provides fillable forms and an interactive form interview. Form 3.1 is the ordinary in-state money-judgment petition; choose the appropriate alternative for nonresident defendants or other claim types. Read the instructions for the particular form. The original notice commences the action under § 631.3 [2, 8].

The Judicial Branch currently lists a $115 filing fee; § 631.6 separately states $95 filing/docketing and $20 mailing postage. Confirm the actual charge and service expenses with the clerk rather than assuming all fee components are identical for every service election. An answer has no filing fee. If unable to pay, ask about the official application to defer costs and eligibility; deferral is not necessarily forgiveness.

EDMS is Iowa’s electronic filing system. Register, choose the correct county and small claims case type, complete the original notice, select the authorized service method, submit the documents and pay required charges. Paper filing requires court authorization where applicable. Save submission receipts and accepted file-stamped documents. Check acceptance or rejection notices; submission alone is not assurance a rejected document will count as filed. See Iowa Rules of Electronic Procedure, chapter 16, and the official eFile resources [8–9].

Write the claim in plain language: who owed what duty, what happened, when it happened, the resulting loss and the relief requested. Use accurate amounts and party names. Keep the narrative relevant. Do not include protected personal identifiers in public filings; follow the current privacy rules and approved confidential filing procedures. Ask the clerk about administrative steps, not how to win your case.

## 4 | Service and deadline management

Service of original notice is distinct from mailing a demand or notifying customer support. Section 631.4 sets the available service methods. For ordinary money or replevin claims, clerk-arranged certified mail under subsection 1(a) or personal service under 1(b) ordinarily gives 20 days following service. Other court-approved nonresident service under 1(c) gives 60 days following service; Secretary of State service under 1(d) gives 60 days from filing with that office. Eviction and abandonment have separate rules. Use the original notice and filed proof of service to establish the relevant date.

Under § 631.5(4), proper notice is established by the required signed receipt or return of service. Check that the correct defendant was served through an authorized recipient and that the return is filed. Failed or disputed service may require correction or another attempt. With multiple defendants, deadlines may differ; § 631.5(3) addresses partial service. If a name or service problem arises, request appropriate court direction rather than assuming a correction is effective.

Time computation: § 4.1(34) generally excludes the triggering day and includes the last day. Intermediate weekends and holidays count for an ordinary 20-calendar-day period. When the final filing day falls on a qualifying weekend, holiday or authorized clerk closure, the statute extends it to the next day the office is open. Consult the court holiday schedule and rule 16.309 regarding electronic timestamps, rejection and system unavailability. File early, retain the receipt and do not assume an outage automatically extends a deadline [9–10].

Illustration: if ordinary personal service occurs on Monday June 1, day one is June 2 and day twenty is June 21. If the final day is Sunday and the clerk is open Monday June 22, the ordinary deadline extends to that Monday. This is a counting illustration, not a ruling about any real case.

## 5 | Responding, default and inactivity

### If you are the defendant

Read the notice immediately, calendar the deadline, file the official Appearance and Answer, and monitor EDMS. A denial does not waive defenses under § 631.5(1). Identify defenses, such as payment, mistaken identity, limitations, lack of jurisdiction or an applicable arbitration clause. A counterclaim requests affirmative relief; an offset arising from the same subject matter does not require a counterclaim under § 631.7(4). That subsection expressly excludes civil rule 1.241 from small claims. Counterclaims against existing parties are deemed denied; no responsive pleading is required under that provision. Seek advice about separate claims and preclusion.

### If no answer appears

A missed response deadline is not itself a judgment. Under § 631.5(6), when the defendant fails to appear and the clerk determines proper notice was given, the clerk enters judgment if relief is readily ascertainable; otherwise the matter is assigned to a judicial magistrate. Provide the required evidence and applicable verification forms, including Form 3.27 where required by the current instructions. Federal military-service protections under 50 U.S.C. § 3931 apply to covered individual defendants; do not guess about status. Follow the clerk’s procedure and any judicial order. Do not treat a late filing before judgment as automatically barred; the court determines its effect.

A pending case can also be dismissed for inactivity: § 631.8(1) provides for dismissal without prejudice when a small claim remains undetermined for 90 days following expiration of a continuance or the last docket entry. Monitor the docket and comply with notices; do not file unnecessary papers merely to manufacture activity.

## 6 | Motions, counterclaims and transfers

Small claims is intentionally simplified. Section 631.7(1) limits written pleadings and motions to those specifically authorized or permitted by the court in the interests of justice. Under § 631.7(2), motions generally are heard at the merits hearing, except the stated rule 1.246 exception. Follow court orders and do not assume ordinary civil motion practice or discovery is available. If you need a threshold ruling, explain the issue concisely and seek appropriate permission or counsel.

A claim exceeding the small claims jurisdictional limit may require regular procedure. Section 631.8(2) gives the court specified options when a small claim is joined with a non-small claim: hear the small claim and dismiss the other without prejudice, hear the claims separately under their proper procedures for qualifying parties, or hear both under regular procedure. A larger counterclaim is not an automatic transfer or proof of bad faith. Section 631.9 requires the court to consider proper notice and whether the action qualifies as a small claim.

## 7 | Prepare for and attend the hearing

Under § 631.11, the judge conducts an informal hearing, swears witnesses and examines the parties to establish the facts. The parties may participate personally or through authorized representation. Informality does not eliminate the need to prove the claim. The usual civil burden is preponderance of the evidence, subject to any different standard for the particular claim.

Prepare a one-page chronology and damages worksheet, followed by clearly numbered exhibits. Each exhibit should support an identifiable disputed fact. Keep originals and readable copies; follow the hearing notice and court directions for electronic exhibit submission, exchange and timing. Redact protected information in public copies. An uploaded exhibit is not necessarily admitted evidence. Ask the court to consider each necessary exhibit, explain what it is and how you know it is accurate, and obtain a ruling where needed. Do not submit repetitive or irrelevant material.

Witnesses: request subpoenas through the clerk or judicial officer under § 631.3(4) when necessary. Confirm service, witness fees and any requirements for documents or remote attendance. A written statement may not substitute for a witness who can be questioned. Ask for an interpreter, disability accommodation or continuance early if needed; attend unless the court grants a change.

At the hearing, follow the judge’s directions. Present the essential facts and requested relief; identify exhibits and page numbers aloud; answer questions directly; let the other side finish; and challenge disputed facts respectfully. Do not argue with witnesses or make personal attacks. If evidence is excluded, politely ask how to preserve the substance for review. Do not independently record the proceeding without authorization.

Contested proceedings are recorded electronically unless a party provides a certified reporter at that party’s expense; see § 631.11(3). Check that the record identifies the exhibits relied upon. Under § 631.10, absent good cause, neither party appearing leads to dismissal without prejudice; plaintiff absent and defendant present leads to dismissal with prejudice and costs; plaintiff present and defendant absent may result in judgment. The statute has a specific verified-account or genuine-instrument appearance provision; do not skip a scheduled hearing without court confirmation.

## 8 | Judgment, review and finality

Read the actual judgment, not merely the docket summary. Note principal, costs, interest, installment terms and any conditions. Under § 631.12, court-ordered installments prevent execution while paid as ordered; an affidavit of default is necessary for enforcement of such a judgment. An adverse decision is not automatically stayed by filing a motion or appeal.

Default relief: § 631.12(2) incorporates Iowa R. Civ. P. 1.977. A motion to set aside default must be made promptly after discovery of the grounds and no later than 60 days after entry. Good cause may include mistake, inadvertence, surprise, excusable neglect or unavoidable casualty. The outcome depends on the facts and applicable case law; seek counsel promptly.

Ordinary appeal: under § 631.13(1), a party may announce appeal orally at the conclusion of the hearing or file written notice within 20 days after judgment is rendered. In either event the district court docket fee must be paid within that period to perfect the appeal, subject to any applicable authorized fee relief. Under § 631.13(2), a bond in the judgment amount with approved surety stays execution. Confirm any other stay order before collection.

Appeal ordinarily proceeds on the existing record without additional evidence; the deciding judge may order additional evidence if the record is inadequate (§ 631.13(4)). A magistrate’s decision may be reviewed by a district associate or district judge; a district associate judge’s by a district judge; a district judge’s by another district judge. Section 631.13(3) allows transcript filing within 20 days after appeal unless extended by order or stipulation. Request the official transcript promptly and follow the costs and record procedures.

Further review is discretionary under § 631.16 and Iowa R. App. P. 6.106; ordinarily the application is due within 30 days after the challenged ruling, subject to the rule’s provisions for a timely rule 1.904(2) motion. Obtain legal advice immediately. Do not treat expiration of one review deadline as eliminating every possible challenge or stay. [1, 9]

## 9 | Collecting a money judgment

A judgment establishes an obligation; it does not guarantee money will be available. First confirm enforceability, any stay or installments, the exact outstanding balance and the debtor’s identity. A neutral written payment request with a copy of the judgment can be useful. Record payments and lawful costs, avoid duplicative collection and keep settlement terms in writing. Do not threaten arrest for civil nonpayment.

Interest: §§ 535.3 and 668.13 govern applicable judgment interest. The statutory framework includes a rate based on the one-year Treasury constant maturity rate plus two percentage points, but contractual and other exceptions and accrual rules matter. Use the official court interest table and the judgment’s terms; do not automatically change a judgment’s rate each month or assume interest begins only at entry [11].

Execution: review chapters 626 and 642 and the official collection guide. Identify debtor-owned assets and the proper county. Form 3.25 is the Request for General Execution. The clerk issues the execution; the sheriff, not the creditor, carries it out. Advance fees may apply, and recovery of costs depends on law and collection success. The court guide describes a 120-day execution period. A bank account, wage payment or asset may be exempt, jointly owned, subject to other claims, or outside Iowa jurisdiction. Obtain counsel where ownership or interstate enforcement is uncertain [12].

Garnishment is not an instruction to seize any account associated with a trade name. Follow the applicable sheriff, notice, exemption, answer, hearing and condemnation procedures. Chapters 627 and 642 and federal law protect specified property, benefits and earnings. Sections 642.14 and 642.14A have different notice requirements; no single universal ten-day rule covers all garnishments. A natural-person debtor may have a specific notice deadline under § 642.14A. A debtor can contest improper garnishment using the applicable court process, including Form 3.20. Obtain legal advice rather than relying on a generic timetable.

Debtor examination: § 630.1 ordinarily requires an execution returned unsatisfied in whole or part, issued to the county specified by the statute. Section 630.2 permits an alternative after execution issues upon satisfactory proof of property unjustly withheld from payment. The court issues the examination order; service and scope matter. Examination concerns assets available to satisfy the judgment, not relitigation or unrelated investigations. Contempt under § 630.11 concerns failure to comply with a lawful order, not mere inability to pay. Seek counsel for corporate representatives, subpoenas, or cross-border proceedings.

Judgment liens: §§ 624.23–624.24 govern qualifying real estate liens and docketing in other Iowa counties. The usual lien period is ten years; the Judicial Branch identifies twenty years for execution on ordinary small claims judgments, subject to statutory exceptions, enforceability conditions and renewal rules. See §§ 614.1(6), 614.3, 626.2 and 631.12. Homestead protections, competing liens, ownership and bankruptcy can materially change remedies. Do not assume a lien attaches to all assets or survives indefinitely.

## 10 | Satisfaction, closure and public records

After full satisfaction, § 624.37 requires the creditor to execute a duly acknowledged or notarized instrument identifying the judgment and file it in every county where the judgment is a lien. Use official Form 3.23, Release and Satisfaction of Judgment, as appropriate. Confirm principal, awarded costs and applicable interest are satisfied, or that agreed settlement conditions have been fulfilled. A debtor may use Form 3.22 to apply for release and satisfaction when appropriate.

The $400 statutory penalty applies when satisfaction is not acknowledged in the required manner within 30 days after a written request containing a draft release. The original syllabus incorrectly described an ordinary written demand as sufficient and added attorney fees as an automatic statutory entitlement; § 624.37 does not state that additional entitlement. Subsection 2 provides a court-authorized payment route when a creditor cannot be found or is unresponsive, subject to its requirements [13].

Retain the judgment, payment record, accepted satisfaction and any lien releases. Court records may remain accessible subject to retention, confidentiality, sealing and access rules; do not promise permanent online availability of every filing or recording. When sharing a public document, accurately describe its status and avoid presenting allegations as judicial findings. Do not distribute protected identifiers or unrelated private material.

## 11 | Practical worksheets and checklists

### Case file checklist

Before filing: correct parties and venue; limitations and contract review; claim elements; damages ledger; supporting documents; official form; filing and service budget.

After filing: case number; acceptance receipt; service election; return of service; response deadline; calendar and docket monitoring; court orders; required verification.

Before hearing: notice and attendance plan; exhibit index; copies and originals; witness arrangements; damages total; settlement status; redactions; concise testimony.

After judgment: entered judgment; appeal and default-relief deadlines; stay or installments; balance ledger; collection feasibility; payment confirmation; satisfaction and retained records.

### Suggested ledger columns

Date | Event or transaction | Source/exhibit | Amount charged | Amount paid or credited | Remaining principal | Awarded costs | Interest | Notes. Keep disputed charges separate from money actually lost. Reconcile to source records; never overwrite originals.

### Key deadline reference

Ordinary service: 20 days after service (§ 631.4(1)(a)–(b)); specified alternative nonresident service: 60 days under § 631.4(1)(c)–(d). Appeal notice and fee: 20 days after judgment rendered (§ 631.13(1)). Default set-aside: promptly, maximum 60 days after entry (rule 1.977). Discretionary review: ordinarily 30 days (rule 6.106). Satisfaction penalty: 30 days following qualifying written request with draft release (§ 624.37). Verify the triggering event, calendar rules and exceptions in each instance.

## 12 | Special proceedings and when to get help

Eviction: chapter 648 governs grounds, notices, service and hearing deadlines. Replevin: review chapter 643 and chapter 631 for property recovery and related procedure. Abandonment: chapter 555B governs specified property proceedings. This guide identifies these pathways but is not a step-by-step manual for them. Seek specialized assistance for eviction, bankruptcy, government defendants, personal injury, arbitration, disputed service, claims beyond $6,500, counterclaims involving regular civil procedure, contested appeals or complex collection.

Court clerks can provide forms and procedural information but cannot give legal advice. Iowa Legal Aid and the Iowa State Bar Association may help identify resources or counsel. The Iowa People’s Law Library provides accessible legal education [14].
